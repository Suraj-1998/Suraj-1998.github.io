Where to build a generation of fans

Data analytics · Australian Sports Commission scenario · 2025

Sole author

Brisbane hosts the 2032 Games. The question was where a national sporting body should concentrate its effort to build youth engagement — and the honest answer turned out to be narrower than expected.

I analysed AusPlay national participation data across 142 sports, then collected around 400 news articles through the Guardian API to read what the media conversation was actually about.

Pythonscikit-learnKMeansTF-IDFNMFPlotly

View the code and analysis on GitHub →

  • Roughly 84% of estimated youth participation sat inside the top 20 sports. Spreading investment thinly across 142 would have been a mistake.
  • Ranking on raw participation favours whatever is already big, so I engineered measures for youth concentration, adolescent retention and gender balance — with a minimum participation threshold so small sports couldn't produce unstable ratios.
  • Clustering turned four separate rankings into one segmentation a decision maker could act on.

Measuring Brisbane moving, in millimetres

Research project · Sole-authored · 2025

Sole author

Satellite radar can detect ground deformation beneath a city, but published studies often omit the processing settings that produced their results — which makes the results impossible to check. I built a workflow that doesn't have that problem.

An end-to-end pipeline in ESA SNAP converting Sentinel-1 radar imagery into geocoded coherence and displacement maps across three acquisition dates, using only open data and open software.

ESA SNAPSentinel-1 InSARSRTMGeocodingGoogle Earth Pro
  • Detected millimetre-scale deformation across the metropolitan area, with signal holding through the CBD and decorrelating measurably across vegetated outer suburbs.
  • Tested how much orbit precision, baseline length and elevation model choice actually changed the answer, rather than assuming.
  • Documented every processing parameter so the workflow can be re-run by someone else, and delivered outputs as map layers a planner can open without specialist software.

A model I deliberately made less accurate

Introduction to data science · Three-person team · 2025

Data preparation & evaluation

A classification model predicting COVID-19 positivity from a 37-variable health and behavioural survey. The interesting part wasn't the model — it was the two decisions that made it honest.

I led preprocessing: correcting data types, standardising inconsistent missing-value placeholders, and applying mode and median imputation so outliers didn't drag the fill values.

Pythonpandasscikit-learnseaborn
  • One predictor correlated with the target at 0.92. Leaving it in would have produced an excellent accuracy score and a useless model, so it came out of the feature set.
  • Comparing a decision tree against k-nearest neighbours, I chose the model that generalised better rather than the one with the higher headline number.
  • Kept some skewness on purpose — removing it would have cost more records than the modelling could spare.
